\section{Causes}
This condition happens because of spores being inhaled (rhinocerebral, lung) or inoculated through skin, with the fungus being angioinvasive, causing blood clot formation, tissue tissue death due to lack of blood supply, and bleeding. The underlying mechanisms involve complex interactions between genetic predisposition and environmental factors that disrupt normal physiological processes. \section{Risk Factors}
Mucormycosis is a rare, rapidly progressive, life-threatening fungal infection caused by molds of the order Mucorales (\textit{Rhizopus}, \textit{Mucor}, \textit{Lichtheimia}, \textit{Cunninghamella}), with incidence increasing with the rising number of immunocompromised patients, and risk factors including diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA), neutropenia, HSCT/SOT, iron overload, and corticosteroid use. \section{Progression and Stages}
The condition may progress through different stages of severity over time. Early stages often involve mild or subtle symptoms that may be overlooked. Moderate stages involve more noticeable symptoms affecting daily function. Advanced stages may involve significant impairment and complications.

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item You may experience rhinocerebral mucormycosis (most common, particularly in DKA): fever, facial or periorbital pain, nasal congestion, black necrotic eschar, proptosis, vision loss, and cranial nerve palsies
  \item Lung mucormycosis: fever, cough, hemoptysis, and rapid progression.
\end{itemize}

\section{Diagnosis}
Doctors diagnose this using tissue biopsy showing broad, ribbon-like, non-septate hyphae with right-angle branching. \section{Treatment Options}
