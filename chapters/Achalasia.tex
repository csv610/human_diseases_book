\chapter{Achalasia}
\index{Achalasia}
\label{sec:Achalasia}

\section{Overview}
Achalasia is a primary esophageal motility disorder characterized by impaired lower esophageal sphincter (LES) relaxation and absent esophageal peristalsis.
\section{Causes}
This condition results from progressive degeneration of inhibitory nitrergic ganglion cells in the myenteric (Auerbach) plexus of the esophageal wall, leading to impaired lower esophageal sphincter relaxation.
\section{Risk Factors}

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]

  \item Lifestyle factors (diet, exercise, smoking, alcohol)
  \item Environmental exposures
  \item Underlying medical conditions
  \item Medication use
\end{itemize}

\section{Progression and Stages}
This condition happens because of autoimmune deterioration of inhibitory nitrergic ganglion cells in the myenteric (Auerbach) plexus of the esophagus. The condition may progress through different stages of severity over time. Early stages often involve mild or subtle symptoms that may be overlooked. Moderate stages involve more noticeable symptoms affecting daily function. Advanced stages may involve significant impairment and complications.
\section{Complications}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]

  \item Side effects of treatment
  \item Reduced quality of life
  \item Emotional and psychological impact
\end{itemize}

\section{Outlook}
The outlook is good for symptom control, though patients have a slightly increased risk of esophageal squamous cell carcinoma. The outlook varies depending on the specific condition, severity at diagnosis, and response to treatment. Early intervention generally leads to better outcomes. Many conditions can be effectively managed with appropriate treatment. Regular follow-up care is important for monitoring and treatment adjustment.
