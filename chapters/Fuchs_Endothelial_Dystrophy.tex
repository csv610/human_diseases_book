\index{Fuchs' Endothelial Dystrophy}
\label{sec:Fuchs' Endothelial Dystrophy}

\section{Overview}


\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item Fuchs' endothelial corneal dystrophy is a bilateral, progressive disorder of the corneal endothelium characterized by guttae, endothelial cell loss, and corneal swelling
  \item It is autosomal dominant and more common in women.
\end{itemize}
\section{Causes}
This condition happens because of progressive endothelial cell dysfunction and loss.     
\section{Risk Factors}

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item Genetic predisposition and family history
  \item Advancing age
  \item Lifestyle factors (diet, exercise, smoking, alcohol)
  \item Environmental exposures
  \item Underlying medical conditions
  \item Medication use
\end{itemize}

\section{Progression and Stages}
The condition may progress through different stages of severity over time. Early stages often involve mild or subtle symptoms that may be overlooked. Moderate stages involve more noticeable symptoms affecting daily function. Advanced stages may involve significant impairment and complications.

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item Early disease is asymptomatic
  \item As the disease progresses, You may experience morning blurring that improves through the day, glare, and reduced visual acuity
  \item Advanced disease causes persistent corneal swelling and bullous keratopathy.
\end{itemize}
\section{Complications}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item Disease progression leading to worsening symptoms
  \item Secondary infections or injuries
  \item Side effects of treatment
  \item Reduced quality of life
  \item Emotional and psychological impact
\end{itemize}
\section{Diagnosis}
Doctors diagnose this using slit-lamp examination and specular microscopy showing reduced endothelial cell density. \section{Outlook}
Most people recover well with surgical intervention. The outlook varies depending on the specific condition, severity at diagnosis, and response to treatment. Early intervention generally leads to better outcomes. Many conditions can be effectively managed with appropriate treatment. Regular follow-up care is important for monitoring and treatment adjustment.
