\chapter{Iron Overdose}
\index{Iron Overdose}
\label{sec:Iron Overdose}

\section{Overview}


\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item Iron overdose is a leading cause of poisoning death in children under 6 years due to accidental ingestion of prenatal iron supplements. Toxic dose: elemental iron >20 mg/kg
  \item Severe toxicity >60 mg/kg.
\end{itemize}
\section{Causes}
This condition happens because of free iron catalyzing hydroxyl radical formation via the Fenton reaction, causing lipid peroxidation, mitochondrial dysfunction, and metabolic acidosis.     
\section{Risk Factors}

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item Genetic predisposition and family history
  \item Advancing age
  \item Lifestyle factors (diet, exercise, smoking, alcohol)
  \item Environmental exposures
  \item Underlying medical conditions
  \item Medication use
\end{itemize}

\section{Progression and Stages}
The condition may progress through different stages of severity over time. Early stages often involve mild or subtle symptoms that may be overlooked. Moderate stages involve more noticeable symptoms affecting daily function. Advanced stages may involve significant impairment and complications.

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item Clinical stages: Stage 1 (0--6 hours): nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, diarrhea, hematemesis, hematochezia
  \item Stage 2 (6--24 hours): apparent recovery
  \item Stage 3 (12--48 hours): metabolic acidosis, seizures, coma, shock, coagulopathy, liver tissue death, lung swelling
  \item Stage 4 (2--4 weeks): digestive tract obstruction from strictures.
\end{itemize}
\section{Complications}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]

  \item Side effects of treatment
  \item Reduced quality of life
  \item Emotional and psychological impact
\end{itemize}
\section{Diagnosis}
Doctors diagnose this using serum iron level and abdominal X-ray. Treatment: aggressive IV fluids, deferoxamine (IV iron chelator) for metabolic acidosis, shock, or iron level >500 mcg/dL, and whole-bowel irrigation for retained tablets.
